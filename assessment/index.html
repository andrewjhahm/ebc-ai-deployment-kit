Executive Briefing AI Readiness Assessment

Fifteen questions, about five minutes. It scores where your briefing program is ready to apply AI, where it is not yet, and what the first three moves should be.

It is deliberately unglamorous about this. Most briefing teams fail at AI adoption not because the models are weak but because the inputs are scattered, the work is bespoke every time, and nobody has said out loud who signs off. Those are the things this measures.

Automate first

The assembly tax

  • Account snapshots and recent-signal research
  • Attendee background synthesis from public sources
  • Agenda scaffolding from a known meeting type
  • Post-meeting recap drafting and action extraction
  • Asset lookup across the content library

Keep human

The judgment

  • What this specific customer actually needs to hear
  • Which executive takes which part of the room
  • Reading the room and changing course mid-meeting
  • The commitment made on the company's behalf
  • Final sign-off on anything the customer sees

The operating principle: standardize the content, customize the conversation. The prep, the capture, and the follow-through are automatable. The room is not. A deployment that gets this backwards produces briefings that are faster and worse, which is the outcome that ends AI programs.
